A claim, and every way it could be wrong

Three ways to organise the same challenges against the same three claims. The data is shared; only the organising axis differs. Open one, switch claims at the top, take a challenge onto the claim, then flip to as a reader.

B3 · What this run says

The page belongs to a run. It opens with what the run holds, the only evidence the AI reads, then the readings that can be made of it: yours, the AI's, or any text at all. Every phrase lands on a piece of the run or on nothing; severity sits on the underline; approving cites the run and the cell it was read from. On the lobbying run, try the sentence the run cannot back. Single cell claims only; the table claim is parked behind ?tables=1.

What all three share

the unitOne claim at a time. The page is reached from a minted claim, on the run that made it.
what gets a numberA challenge a free rerun can price. This mock proposes only; every figure shown was computed in an earlier session, none here.
the pruning ruleA challenge that does not move the figure at published precision is folded, with the count. The methodology's own settled choices go there too.
the two actionsAdd to the claim writes a qualifier the reader sees. Dismiss removes it and writes nothing, the way local review does.
an alternative claimShown as a claim shape, never minted from here. Claim this beside it stands it next to the first, so the page says which reading the author asserts.
who sees whatThe author sees the list. The reader sees what the author kept. Nothing tells the reader a list existed.

Where they differ

A · LedgerB · The sentenceC · The register
what bounds the countThe published-precision rule alone. The list is as long as what moves the number.The phrases in the sentence. Five words, five slots; the rest fold under each.The stages behind the figure. A stage with nothing on it is a stage with nothing on it.
best caseA claim with one big finding (LDA).A claim whose fight is about meaning (COMPAS).A project with several claims off one pipeline (DOCCS: four figures share three stages).
worst caseTwo challenges of equal weight read as a ranking that is not there.A challenge that hits no word, or hits all of them (the window).A semantic challenge has no stage to sit on; it lands on the last one by default.
the reader's artefactQualifiers on the claim card.Footnotes on the words of the sentence.A register of choices: reading taken, alternative, movement.
swap to the next claimA new list.A new sentence.Most rows are already there.
